Three thousand Canadian arenas run an ice resurfacer. Only some of them are Zambonis.

ZAMBONI has been a registered trademark since 1965, and so has the shape of the machine. Olympia builds in Elmira, Ontario. Okay Elektra builds in Terrebonne, Quebec. Zamboni itself hand-builds in Brantford as well as California. It is not a Canadian invention, but it is very much a Canadian industry.

A few things we found counting it properly:

The water goes down at 62 degrees. Hot water melts into the layer underneath instead of sitting on top of it, so the two freeze as one sheet. Cold water sets as a skin and chips off by the second period.

Every pass takes 2,500 lb of snow off the ice and lays 665 litres of water behind it. Times 2,595 passes a season, that is 1.7 million litres of hot water in a single arena.

The machine weighs 4,423 kg empty and 5,148 kg full. It gets heavier as it works.

A new Olympia ran Minto, Ontario $117,944 in 2024. The electric version costs about $50,000 more and saves up to 80 percent on fuel. 18 percent of Ontario operators run electric today. Another 45 percent say they will inside five years.

And the number that started this: one resurface costs about $13.86. Operator time is half of it. The hour of prime ice it makes sellable goes for $385.95 in Oakville.

The ten minutes nobody pays for is what makes the fifty sellable. That is usually where the margin is hiding.
